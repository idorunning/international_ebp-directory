## Source descriptions and translations

Each addition preserves its original institutional/publication name and `originalLanguage` (BCP 47 code), plus concise English, French and Spanish directory descriptions under `translations`. These are AI-assisted editorial descriptions of the destination. They do not translate the research, imply that underlying papers exist in all three languages, or assert reviewed findings. `languages` records content actually observed or confirmed in official indexed publication records. Traditional Chinese uses the existing search taxonomy label “Mandarin Chinese”; the script distinction is preserved by `originalLanguage: zh-Hant` and the access note.

Native names and translated source metadata should be searchable alongside the English description. A source-language filter concerns the source's actual publication language, not the chosen website interface language. No full external research text has been copied into the directory index.

## Discovery coverage and exclusions

Searches covered Spanish-language university citizen-security collections, Portuguese violence/policing research, Korean police research reports, Taiwanese police university publications, Nigerian accountability research and French-language Senegalese safeguarding research. Japanese police/criminology publication routes were also explored but no sufficiently inspectable collection was added in this pass.

CRISP/UFMG was not added because the intended collection could not be reliably retrieved. Colombia's Logos journal was not added because the official publication endpoint was inaccessible and the results obtained were predominantly copies on external platforms. Ryukoku's criminology centre was not added because its landing page exposed no substantive publication content to the retrieval tool. These exclusions are discovery/access limits, not assessments of research quality.

No automatic ownership, high-quality evidence, causal impact or UK transferability has been inferred from these organisations' names. Publication-level methods, relevance and access still need checking when a reader chooses a study.
