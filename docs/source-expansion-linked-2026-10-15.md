# Linked policing sources and public research writing

Reviewed 15 October 2026 (UK local date). Targeted discovery following the SEBP website, comparable academic/practitioner networks, named Substack and Medium publications, and public police evaluation collections. This is a source-directory review, not a systematic review of policing interventions or a quality appraisal of every linked study.

## Selection and provenance

Twelve proposed records are in `.work/sources-linked.json`. IDs and destination URLs were checked against the existing 94-record catalogue. No record represents a confirmed member, partnership or verified owner. All use editorial provenance, empty community signals and `listed` status. Public email addresses were not used to imply that anybody has registered or approved a listing.

The named source is the unit of listing. Substack and Medium are publishing platforms, not evidence authorities. The same author’s newsletter, reports and personal website are kept together rather than counted as separate independent sources. Publication links and verification URLs retain the route to the original work.

## SEBP and related network routes actually followed

- [SEBP’s EBP guide](https://www.sebp.police.uk/allaboutebp) links to the College of Policing, CEBCP material and named authors’ books. The relevant institutional sources already exist in the catalogue, so they were not added again.
- [SEBP’s 2024 research round-up](https://www.sebp.police.uk/post/10-things-evidence-based-policing-research-taught-us-in-2024) links to original reports, journal articles and institutional publications rather than a single hidden research archive. Its link to the [King’s-hosted police training report](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/policy-institute/assets/the-impact-of-training-on-use-of-force-by-police.pdf) was followed. The article is a discovery lead, not a reason to create duplicate records for each underlying study. No effect-size claim from the round-up is imported into the directory.
- [SEBP’s Flashlight account](https://www.sebp.police.uk/post/flashlight-shines-a-light-on-policing-innovation) links to a Police Superintendents’ Association news account. A competition news item was not reclassified as a research publication collection.
- N8 and the BSC Policing Network provide additional named researchers and original publication links. Their existence does not demonstrate partnership with ID-EBP.
- [Shjarback’s recommendations](https://johnshjarback.substack.com/recommendations) provided named newsletter leads, including Jeff Asher. These were followed to actual source pages rather than adding a generic Substack entry.

## Screened-out or deferred leads

- [West Midlands Police research and analysis](https://www.westmidlands.police.uk/police-forces/west-midlands-police/areas/about-us/about-us/research-and-analysis/) is an authentic research collaboration/application route. The inspected page did not provide a research output collection, so it was not padded into this publication-focused expansion.
- Generic Medium and Substack searches returned many opinion, politics, lecture and general-interest pages. Platform membership is not an inclusion criterion.
- Other identifiable authors, including Alex Vitale, James Treadwell and Jeffrey Ian Ross, were discovered. This targeted pass did not add them merely because they have academic credentials or a newsletter; closer relevance/scope assessment remains possible. Exclusion here is not a judgement on their academic work.
- Operational training newsletters were not prioritised for this community/research directory expansion. No photographs or operational material were imported.
- No source was copied in full, paywall bypassed, account created or contact sent.

## Translations and integration

Each new record includes English, French and Spanish `translations` objects containing only the directory name and short editorial description. These are AI-assisted profile translations; they do not translate the linked research or claim official publisher approval. `languages` records the actual source language and is not inflated by translated directory descriptions. Original Dutch/German source names and language codes are retained in `originalName` and `originalLanguage`.

No new country or language mapping is required: United Kingdom (GB), United States (US), Netherlands (NL), Germany (DE); English (en), Dutch (nl), German (de). French (fr) and Spanish (es) are profile-translation/interface codes. All proposed type, content, method and topic values use the existing taxonomy.

The central catalogue and generated assets are deliberately left to the integration owner. Rebuild the metadata index and database seed after merging the twelve proposed records. Catalogue counts should use unique records; linked papers, translations and alternative publishing platforms must not increase the source count.
